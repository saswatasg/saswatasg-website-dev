Enterprise interest in AI is broad. A useful product direction needs a more concrete starting point: a workflow, a constraint and a reason to change it. Discovery helps define that starting point. Delivery tests whether the proposed solution can become something the client can use.

## What I worked on

I built the discovery practice from scratch, including structured client conversations, a survey with 500+ respondents and a framework for organizing evidence into priorities.

The work also included 20+ prospective-client conversations across 12 verticals and a top-three opportunity brief. Those counts describe research scope. They are not a combined sample, and the brief is a prioritization output rather than evidence that three products launched.

Alongside discovery, I derived solutions and managed their delivery end to end for 23+ clients. I coordinated sprint planning and backlogs with a six-developer team, without formal reporting authority.

## What the evidence needs to explain

An interview count is useful context, but it does not explain a product decision by itself. A decision needs the problem, the evidence supporting it, the alternatives and the constraints.

The following is a practical checklist for reviewing an opportunity brief—not a reproduction of a particular interview script or a confidential client artifact:

- **Problem:** Which workflow is difficult, and for whom?
- **Evidence:** What was observed directly, and what remains an assumption?
- **Value:** What changes if the problem is solved?
- **Feasibility:** What data, integration or operational constraints matter?
- **Next step:** What should be investigated, demonstrated or delivered next?

The distinction between evidence and assumption matters throughout the process. A stated interest is different from budget approval. A positive demo reaction is different from production acceptance. A forecast is different from realized revenue.

## Carrying the solution into delivery

Discovery and delivery belong in the same professional story because a proposed solution still needs definition and execution. My Upcore work includes both deriving solutions and managing their delivery for 23+ clients.

[Inventory Leveling & Procurement Intelligence](/case-studies/upcore-inventory-leveling) is a public artifact within that work. The client demo connects orders, component requirements, stock and lead times to procurement planning. Its interface and calculation tests make the solution inspectable.

The [public repository and test documentation](https://github.com/saswatasg/inventory-leveling-agent) describe the implementation. It uses mock data and has no live ERP integration. That is its delivery stage, not a weakness to hide or a production outcome to invent. The artifact demonstrates a workflow and validated calculations; deployment and business impact require additional evidence.

## Measure the right kind of result

For this work, three categories should remain distinct:

| Category | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Research | Scope, evidence and prioritization outputs |
| Client delivery | Responsibility for defining and carrying solutions through delivery |
| Business impact | Measured changes with a baseline, window and attribution |

A strong case study connects these categories where the evidence supports it. Its strongest evidence is the decision trail: what changed, why it changed and what the team delivered.
