DhanPlan is an India-focused retirement-planning product. The current story is a monthly model connecting savings, assets and retirement withdrawals, with assumptions and limitations made visible. A projection is a scenario, not a guarantee or proof of financial-model accuracy.

## A plan needs more than a final number

A retirement target is useful only when someone can understand the path to it. Contributions occur over time, different instruments have different rules, assets may mature at different points and spending continues after retirement. A single compound-growth figure cannot explain all of those interactions.

DhanPlan’s product direction is to connect those elements in one plan. The interface needs to communicate which inputs drive the result and which changes are meaningful. Showing a large future corpus without explaining inflation or withdrawals would make the output easier to misread.

## Model the sequence month by month

The published methodology for Engine 2.0.0 describes a monthly ledger with contributions, balances, instrument behavior and retirement withdrawals. The rules review date is 2 October 2026. Instruments include EPF, PPF, NPS, deposits, precious metals and property-related cash flows alongside investment assumptions.

The point of a shared ledger is consistency across the plan. A maturity event after retirement still matters; an asset should not silently disappear because it falls outside the contribution phase. Versioning also gives a correction a visible context instead of presenting every output as timeless truth.

## Make the gap actionable

The gap-solving feature reruns the model to explore the contribution change needed under the selected assumptions. It is different from promising that a particular investment will achieve the target. It depends on the same return, inflation, timing and cash-flow choices as the underlying projection.

Nominal and inflation-adjusted views answer different questions. The former describes future currency amounts; the latter helps a person think about purchasing power. An understandable product keeps that distinction visible rather than switching between them without explanation.

## Stress the assumptions, not just the interface

The documented stress scenarios include higher and lower assumptions and 1,000 Monte Carlo paths. These show how sensitive a plan can be to the selected inputs. Simulated paths are conditional on their modelling assumptions; they are not a complete picture of every possible future event.

A helpful experience should let a user see which assumption changed and how the result moved. It should also explain when a feature is a deterministic calculation and when it is a simulated distribution. More scenarios are not automatically better if their meaning is hard to understand.

## Keep limitations beside the model

The methodology documents limits around fees, exit costs, some tax timing and other instrument or household details. A calculator cannot infer every future household decision from a short form. The product should make exclusions discoverable and date-sensitive rules traceable to the methodology.

This portfolio does not claim independently verified financial accuracy, a fabricated test count or measured adoption. Those claims would need their own evidence. The delivered artifact is a live planning interface and a documented model; further verification should use reconciled fixtures and reproducible scenarios.

The broader product lesson is to treat assumptions as part of the interface. A user should be able to inspect what a number means, change its inputs and understand where the model stops. See [DhanPlan](https://www.dhanplan.in/) and its [current methodology](https://www.dhanplan.in/methodology) for the versioned rules and limitations.
