## Write the decision before opening the dashboard

Begin with the choice the team needs to make. Is a workflow failing, is a feature being bypassed, or is the measured population wrong? A question such as “why is conversion low?” is too broad until its entry event, completion event and qualifying users are defined. Decide what evidence would change the recommendation. This keeps a visually interesting chart from becoming the answer by default.

## Check the denominator and the event

Before interpreting a decline, inspect whether the same action is counted consistently across devices and releases. Distinguish people, sessions, leads and transactions. Missing events, repeated submits and changes in consent can alter the apparent funnel. Keep an explicit note of the comparison period and known instrumentation gaps. A precise-looking percentage can still describe the wrong thing.

## Connect the aggregate to a real journey

A funnel identifies where the measured flow narrows. Session evidence and workflow conversations help explain what may be happening there. Neither is sufficient alone: a recording shows one experience, while an aggregate can conceal several different experiences. Google’s [funnel exploration guide](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9327974) describes the measurement tool. The [Clarity heatmap documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/heatmaps/heatmaps-overview) describes behavioral views that can complement it.

## Compare explanations instead of collecting opinions

Keep a short hypothesis list. For each explanation, record the supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, uncertainty and the cheapest useful next check. A validation problem, an unclear proposition and an existing external workflow need different responses. Prioritization should consider user impact and delivery constraints, not merely the number of people who favor an idea. A hypothesis is not a proven cause until an appropriate evaluation supports it.

## Leave the team with an actionable record

The output should state the decision, scope, owner, success measure and unresolved questions. The [LiveKeeping adoption investigation](/case-studies/livekeeping-compliance-gap) shows workflow diagnosis in a specific setting; [checkout work](/case-studies/cart-checkout) shows buying-journey friction. Their reported numbers belong to their individual cases. Reusing the method does not imply that every new analysis will produce the same uplift.
