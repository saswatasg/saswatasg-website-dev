## One rate, several possible explanations

A checkout abandonment rate does not explain the interaction that caused someone to leave. Cost clarity, validation and payment recovery can create friction at different points. Treating them as one undifferentiated problem makes it difficult to choose a useful change.

My contribution was to connect the measured journey with the actual checkout experience, then translate the findings into targeted improvements with the team. The scope included **480,000 sessions**, GA4 funnel instrumentation and Microsoft Clarity evidence.

## Diagnose the step before choosing the solution

A useful investigation defines the entry event, completion event and population before interpreting a drop-off. A cart visitor and someone who has begun checkout are different populations. Device segments matter too: a field can behave acceptably on desktop while creating friction on a small screen.

The working approach combined step-level measurement with session inspection. Analytics showed where the measured flow narrowed; session evidence helped identify plausible interaction problems. A recording explains an individual experience rather than proving that every abandoned session had the same cause.

Google’s [funnel exploration documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9327974) explains how to define and inspect a measured journey.

## Make the next action easier to complete

The work focused on clearer checkout interactions within the existing storefront. The relevant design concerns included cost visibility, validation feedback and payment-error recovery. Each needs its own acceptance criteria: can a customer understand the amount, correct an input and continue after an error?

This is also where product management connects diagnosis with delivery. A finding must become a bounded implementation decision, a shared requirement and a way to check whether the change behaves as intended on mobile.

## Keep the outcome and its limits clear

| Reported measure               | Result            |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Abandonment                    | 73.1% → 53.9%     |
| Relative abandonment reduction | Approximately 26% |
| Mobile conversion rate         | +47%              |
| Checkout completion            | +49%              |
| Analysis scope                 | 480,000 sessions  |

The supplied record reports these outcomes. The public article does not reproduce a randomized experiment, raw event export or full attribution model. The percentages describe different metrics and should not be summed, or converted into an unsupported revenue estimate. External cart-abandonment benchmarks also use populations that may not match this checkout.

## The reusable lesson

The useful habit is to move from a broad metric to a specific decision: define the journey, inspect the friction, agree a change and evaluate it against a stable definition. Preserve a guardrail for errors and customer confidence as well as conversion.
