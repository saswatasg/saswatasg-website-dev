## A category page has a job to do

A product grid is useful when the customer already knows how to choose. A considered furniture purchase can require more context: which options fit the need, what customization involves and why the next step is worth taking. The product question was how the category journey could support that choice. More content is not automatically more help; its position and relevance need to be tied to the customer’s decision.

## Bring behavior and event data together

Event analysis helps locate the transition from category exploration to product detail or an enquiry. Heatmaps and recordings add evidence about what people encounter along that path. The data does not explain intent by itself, and a handful of sessions does not prove how the whole audience behaves. The useful work is to connect those perspectives into a clear problem statement and a prioritized change.

## Make the next step understandable

The redesign replaced a commodity presentation with a more contextual, trust-led journey. A stronger page helps a visitor understand the category, inspect relevant products and choose what to do next. This is a description of the product direction, not a claim that one particular layout suits every category. Google’s [funnel exploration guide](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9327974) and Microsoft’s [Clarity documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/heatmaps/heatmaps-overview) provide the measurement-tool context.

## Read the reported results carefully

The supplied work record reports +17% session-to-product-detail clicks, +34% qualified leads, −16% bounce and +27% add-to-cart. These measures describe different stages of the journey. They should not be added together, and a +34% qualified-lead figure should not become a claim of +34% revenue. The public case does not reproduce raw cohort data, a randomization record or a complete attribution analysis.

## What would make the evidence stronger

An anonymized event dictionary, comparison dates and a clear definition of a qualified lead would let a reader assess the result more precisely. It would also help distinguish this initiative from other product-page work reporting an add-to-cart improvement. The defensible story is the analytics-led redesign and its reported outcomes, with their scope kept explicit.
