At Sierra Living Concepts, I designed a Gold/Silver/Bronze lead-allocation system using source performance, agent capacity and escalation rules. The observed conversion data informed the design. The target of 5.2 to 3.5 days to close is a pilot objective, not a demonstrated result.

## The decision behind the queue

The work addressed how a four-agent team should allocate leads arriving from six sources. Giving every source the same handling path ignores differences in intent and conversion. A useful routing system needs to translate those differences into a next action without making the source label permanent.

The available analysis reports website-form conversion at 63.5%, chat at 4.7% and cart-abandonment leads at 0.4%. These are observed source rates in the analysed dataset. They do not establish a universal probability of closing the next individual lead, and low-volume categories need particular care.

## Three tiers, different handling

Gold prioritizes high-intent sources for specialist attention. Silver provides a structured follow-up path for intermediate intent. Bronze reduces repetitive manual handling while retaining a route to human review when stronger intent appears.

The important product choice is to make the next action clear. A tier alone is not enough: the team needs assignment rules, capacity handling and escalation conditions. A response indicating a substantial custom order can justify reconsidering a low initial priority. This is operational decision logic, not a learned model being claimed to predict every outcome.

## Capacity changes the routing decision

Availability and agent specialization matter alongside source performance. The allocation design considered day-by-day coverage and a specialist path for high-intent enquiries. Those rules should be evaluated against both response delay and conversion; waiting for a specialist is not automatically better than a prompt response.

The earlier article claimed a measured 25% advantage from delaying a particular day's leads. That comparison is not supported by the supplied record. It has been removed rather than generalized into advice. The [Salesforce guide to lead management](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/lead-management/) provides broader process context, not validation of this case's results.

## Targets are not outcomes

The supplied case describes a 30-day pilot progressing toward rollout. The design included targets for routing accuracy, automation coverage, specialist lead allocation and reducing days to close from 5.2 to 3.5. A target remains a target until a comparable post-change measurement is available.

The earlier portfolio presented 90% routing accuracy, 90% chat automation and increased daily specialist leads as achieved results. This revision treats them as pilot objectives. It also removes a dollar-saving estimate presented as if the money had been recovered. Expected conversion revenue is a scenario, not realized savings.

## The evidence that would close the loop

A stronger follow-up would report assignment compliance, response time, escalation errors and conversion by source and handler over a defined period. Lead quality and seasonal changes need to be accounted for when comparing before and after. The team also needs to know whether the system improved workload balance rather than only moving one metric.

This case establishes an allocation design grounded in observed source differences, with an explicit measurement plan. It does not claim a causal increase in revenue or a verified reduction in days to close.
