## The workflow behind the interface

Outreach is a sequence of states rather than a single text-generation task. A campaign needs to know what has been attempted, what remains pending, which connection requests have changed status and whether a follow-up should be scheduled. Losing those distinctions can create duplicate or badly timed actions.

The repository includes task handlers for connections, follow-ups and pending-request checks, alongside campaign records and background execution. Django provides the application and persistence layer; Playwright operates the browser. The language model participates in the workflow, but the product is not simply a prompt wrapped in a button.

## State is a product requirement

A useful task record should distinguish intended work from attempted work and confirmed completion. Browser activity can fail in ways that are not equivalent: navigation may fail before an action, an action may complete before confirmation is recorded, or a platform state may change between checks.

The project includes reconciliation and failed-state handling. Those mechanisms are important implementation artifacts. They do not establish that every edge case is covered or that the platform will continue to accept the same interaction behavior. A reliable claim needs reproducible tests and operational evidence, not only the presence of task code.

## Correcting the approval claim

The public connection-task handler can perform the send action. The implementation reviewed here does not establish a mandatory human decision before each individual send. The earlier portfolio description of an always-reviewed drafting agent overstated that boundary.

A review-first workflow would be a meaningful future product change: persist a proposed action, display its recipient and content, obtain approval, then execute and record the result. That is a recommendation, not an existing capability being claimed here.

## Platform constraints shape the product

An external platform is more than a browser surface. Its policies, account permissions and interface changes constrain what a product can responsibly offer. LinkedIn documents restrictions around prohibited software and automated activity in its [official help guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387).

This project should therefore remain an archived technical experiment rather than a promise of dependable recruiter outreach. The portfolio does not claim policy compliance, zero account risk, universal compatibility or an unattended service guarantee. Those would require evidence beyond a working browser task.

## What is and is not measured

The public record does not provide a traceable reply-rate or meeting dataset. Repository history and release counts show development activity; they are not proof of user value or conversion improvement.

A stronger future evaluation would measure task-state correctness, duplicate prevention, recovery behavior and the distinction between drafted and executed actions. Any user-outcome study would also need a defined sample, time window and attribution method. Until then, the defensible contribution is workflow engineering and an explicit account of its limitations.

The [TGB Hunt repository](https://github.com/saswatasg/TGBhunt) contains the implementation. It is presented here as an archive, with no invented business result.
