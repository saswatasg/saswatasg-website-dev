## Ask where the work already happens

A feature can be available to a paying customer without becoming their preferred workflow. For an accounting product, the surrounding system matters: invoices, bookkeeping and compliance activities are connected. Looking only at the native feature funnel can miss the substitute workflow a customer already uses.

My work combined analysis of API transactions, product usage and workflow context. The purpose was to understand why users with access to native modules were still generating compliance documents elsewhere. This is a product-discovery problem before it is a feature-redesign problem.

## Keep the ratios tied to their scope

The supplied work record reports a 17:1 E-Way Bill gap and a 19:1 E-Invoice gap, comparing external and native activity. These ratios describe the investigated usage context; they are not a market-wide share estimate or proof that seventeen times as many distinct customers rejected the product.

The underlying cohort table, time window and denominator are not reproduced publicly here. That limits how far a reader should generalize the result. A stronger evidence pack would show the qualifying subscriber cohort, event definitions, comparison period and treatment of repeated transactions.

## Translate the finding into a decision

The analysis was presented at VP and CEO level and contributed to the roadmap discussion. The important change was the framing: low feature usage needed to be understood alongside the workflow customers were already completing outside the native interface.

A connector, data-transfer change or revised entry point could be options to investigate. This case does not assert that a particular middleware implementation was built, installed by 50,000 merchants or achieved a latency target. Those were unsupported additions in the earlier article and have been removed.

## Separate adoption from reliability

A parallel workstream analysed 100K+ API transaction logs and addressed compliance-journey errors. The supplied record reports a 58% reduction in rejection rate for that reliability work. This is a different measure from adoption: fewer rejected submissions do not establish that users moved their workflow into the native module.

Keeping those results separate makes the case more useful. One stream asks why customers use another path; the other asks what fails when a compliance transaction is attempted. They may influence the same roadmap, but their outcomes should not be added together or treated as evidence of the same intervention.

## What the public case establishes

The defensible contribution is analysis, problem framing and an executive narrative connecting feature usage to real workflows. It also shows the importance of distinguishing a diagnostic finding from a delivered solution. Further rollout claims would require release records, cohort adoption data and a measurement window.

For the product context, see the official [E-Way Bill portal](https://ewaybillgst.gov.in/) and the [full compliance-discovery case](/case-studies/livekeeping-compliance-gap). The lesson is to measure the work customers are completing, not simply the interface they are declining to use.
