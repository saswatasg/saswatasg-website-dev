## Choose a decision-sized problem

Start with a specific point in the journey rather than a broad redesign brief. A field validation error, unclear next action or mismatch between an advertisement and its landing page can become a bounded hypothesis. The intervention should be small enough to explain. It also needs to matter: changing a decorative detail without evidence of user friction may produce activity without improving the product.

## Write the hypothesis and the guardrails

A useful hypothesis names the affected users, the change and the expected behavior. Define the main outcome alongside a guardrail such as qualified-lead quality, completion errors or support burden. A higher submission count can be a poor result if the team receives unusable enquiries. Capture the measurement window before launch so the evaluation does not move opportunistically toward whichever metric looks favorable.

## Match the evaluation to the available traffic

A weekly planning rhythm does not guarantee that a result can be measured in seven days. The required duration depends on traffic, effect size, seasonality and the chosen design. Google’s [funnel exploration documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9327974) is useful for understanding the measured journey, but a funnel comparison alone does not establish causality. Distinguish an observed change from an experiment capable of attributing it.

## Keep a decision log, including failures

Record what shipped, what changed, what stayed uncertain and whether the team retained or reversed the intervention. A null result can be useful if it narrows the explanation. A failed change should not disappear from the learning record. The purpose is to make the next decision better, rather than celebrate a growing count of releases. Fixes with an obvious correctness requirement also need not be forced into a conversion experiment.

## Use the loop without promising the outcome

The [checkout case](/case-studies/cart-checkout), [lead-form work](/case-studies/lead-form) and [category-page case](/case-studies/category-discovery) show distinct initiatives. Their reported outcomes should not be summed into a universal weekly growth claim. The reusable discipline is narrower: identify friction, make a clear change and evaluate it at the right scope.
