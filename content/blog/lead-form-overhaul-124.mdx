## Start with the interaction, not the form length

An aggregate completion rate tells a team that people are leaving. It does not tell them which interaction needs attention. The useful question was where users hesitated or failed: field entry, validation, submission, or the next step.

GA4 field-level events and Microsoft Clarity session evidence helped connect the measured funnel with the experience of filling out the form. The project record attributes 23% of drop-offs to a restrictive field. That finding supported a focused intervention rather than a blanket recommendation to remove fields.

## Make the expected action understandable

The redesign addressed three connected needs:

| Change                  | User need                                                  |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Visible format guidance | Understand what the field accepts before an error          |
| Inline validation       | Recognize and correct a problem while entering information |
| Context-specific labels | Understand why the information is needed                   |

Category-specific modules made the form more relevant to the enquiry. A customer asking about custom furniture should encounter a form that supports that task. Faster initialization and mobile interaction details also mattered: a clear field cannot help if it appears unresponsive.

## Evaluate the complete journey

The project record reports **+124% submissions**, **−41% mobile completion time** and **−68% rage clicks**. These outcomes describe different aspects of the experience. More submissions should also be evaluated against lead quality; faster completion should not depend on confusing defaults or skipped information.

A separate role-level record reports conversion moving from **2.14% to 4.40%**, approximately **+105%**. The available records do not establish a shared denominator and comparison window. I keep these figures separate rather than present them as consecutive stages of the same experiment.

For implementation guidance, [web.dev’s form curriculum](https://web.dev/learn/forms) covers input behavior, validation and accessibility.

## What I would make inspectable next

An anonymized field-event dictionary, before-and-after screenshots and a qualified-lead comparison would strengthen the public evidence. The reported results describe the work record; the raw event export is not reproduced here.

For another form, I would begin with the same diagnostic discipline: define completion, inspect field errors, watch mobile interactions and choose a bounded change. Removing fields can be useful, but only when the evidence supports it. See the [full lead-form case](/case-studies/lead-form) and [checkout investigation](/blog/checkout-abandonment-73-to-54).
